## What is a digital garden?

A digital garden (or at least my digital garden) is a place that sits below a fully polished blog while sharing many of its qualities. Unlike a blog pages _can_ and _should_ be edited after publishing.

## Why digital gardening?

With a more traditional blog, the expectation is for each post to be a fully grown, and stand on its own like an impressive, large pine tree. A digital garden can still have these large trees, but can also have small saplings, knotted rhizomes that create more than the sum of their parts, and a few weeds that haven't been cleared yet.

Personally, I tend to have 3-4 side projects going at once in various states of completion. Digital gardening allows me to put these out there even if they don't end up fully formed.

## Virtues

These are some guiding principles and virtues I strive for within my digital garden.

### Imperfect action over inaction

Analysis paralysis is something to avoid as much as possible. The "move fast and break things" motto applies here. Publishing an imperfect, unedited version of something here is better than publishing nothing due to perfectionism.

### Rhizomatic structure

Ideas should be linked (preferably hyperlinked) together to create a knotted structure. Notes across disparate disciplines should be connected to create new concepts.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizome\_(philosophy)
The term rhizomatic refers to _rhizomes,_ a type of plant with no definite center. I'm using the term in roughly the same sense as the philosophers Deleuze and Guattari use it.![[Attachments/rhizome.jpg]]

### Terseness and density in near-finished works

Concepts should be explained with the minimum words necessary where possible. A good heuristic for whether a piece of writing is 'finished' is the input:output words ratio, or _density_. e.g. if 10,000 words are written about a concept that turns into a 1,000 word post, this would be 10:1. The 10,000 words have been turned into a denser 1,000 words like coal into diamonds. Assuming input words are all real unique thoughts, higher density == better end result.

Obviously this conflicts with the first virtue, but this only applies to finished or near finished work.
