## What is a digital garden?

A digital garden (or at least my digital garden) is a place that sits below a fully polished blog while sharing many of its qualities. Unlike a blog pages _can_ and _should_ be edited after publishing.

## Why digital gardening?

For me:

- I have lots of side-projects that don't end up coalescing into a finished product. Digital gardening allows this in-progress work to be put somewhere so this doesn't feel like wasted effort.
- Publishing notes here feels less 'official' than a blog or substack, and the expectation is for them to be unfinished.
- Putting out half finished ideas acts as a soft accountability mechanism pushing me to finish them.
- Most of my research is already in obsidian and publishing is made easy.
- Can switch to hosting on GitHub pages semi-easily if limits on forestry.md are annoying.

## Virtues

These are the guiding principles and virtues I strive for within my digital garden.

### Imperfect action over inaction

Analysis paralysis is something to avoid as much as possible. The "move fast and break things" motto applies here. Publishing an imperfect, unedited version of something here is better than publishing nothing due to perfectionism.

### Rhizomatic structure

Ideas should be linked (preferably hyperlinked) together to create a knotted structure. Notes across disparate disciplines should be connected to create new concepts.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizome\_(philosophy)
The term rhizomatic refers to _rhizomes,_ a type of plant with no definite center.![[Attachments/rhizome.jpg]]

### Terseness and density in near-finished works

Concepts should be explained with the minimum words necessary where possible. A good heuristic for whether a piece of writing is 'finished' is the input:output words ratio, or _density_. e.g. if 10,000 words are written about a concept that turns into a 1,000 word post, this would be 10:1. The 10,000 words have been turned into a denser 1,000 words like coal into diamonds. Assuming input words are all real unique thoughts, higher density == better end result.

Obviously this conflicts with the first virtue, but this only applies to finished or near finished work.

## How would I make my own digital garden?

1. Use [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/). Even if you don't start a digital garden its the best personal note taking app available.
2. Host your notes somewhere. I'm currently using [forestry.md](https://forestry.md/) but there's a high chance I switch to [Quartz](https://quartz.jzhao.xyz/) hosted on Github Pages sometime in the near future.
3. Start writing and eventually publishing. Most setups probably need a flag set in the frontmatter to publish online and this is sensible. Learn how to use obsidian, probably base your vault on [this post](https://stephango.com/vault) by its creator. There are very few good resources on how to use Obsidian properly, lots of slop.
